D2 makes private copies of these already-built, hash-verified OpenSSL lanes;
it does not claim to have rebuilt OpenSSL. Historical nested seals retain
their original bytes and paths. The relocated verifier authenticates the
outer seal, the exact nested member set and current member hashes, the
entire installed-prefix file/directory/symlink inventory, the release tar,
and the reused native EVP executable/data. Positive and tamper controls
exercise this relocation rule without executing modified library copies.

V2 provider builds are fresh and offline: Rust 1.98.0 on the recorded native
x86-64 toolchain, O3/ThinLTO/CGU1, panic unwind, overflow checks on in every
target crate, native C O3. The success markers record compiler invocations.
A manifest-derived canonical source location removes Cargo path-identity
drift; independent target directories must produce byte-identical ordinary
and TLS DSOs. All actual versions, flags, commands and binary hashes are in
the receipts. Historical X301-v1 comparison builds retain C O0 and their
crypto-bigint overflow-off exception, explicitly separated from v2 policy.

Codegen rules reuse the reviewed Phase-C/D1 instruction and call-closure
checks, now applied to the linked DSOs. Taint and timing also use actual
provider paths. ASan/UBSan instrument native C, not stable Rust or the sealed
OpenSSL libraries; whole-process Memcheck is the complementary native lane.
These observations are specific to their recorded binaries and machine.
No historical AArch64 result is reassigned to v2.

Reports and handoff indices are derived after execution. They are not
self-referential build inputs. The frozen input snapshots, compiled-input
manifests, per-stage receipts and final archive seal preserve that distinction.
